## Teaching

My university teaching began at the University of Michigan, where I worked as a
Graduate Student Instructor from 2005 to 2011. I have taught at DGIST since
2015. My courses span calculus, differential equations, linear algebra,
engineering mathematics, geometry, topology, differential geometry, matrix
groups, and quantum computing.

I develop project-based lessons in which students use programming,
visualization, and mathematical modelling to turn an abstract idea into
something they can test and explain. This work has included curriculum projects
for multivariable calculus, engineering mathematics, linear algebra, geometry,
and quantum computing, as well as educational work with virtual reality. I
received DGIST's **Best Mentor Award in 2016**.

## Research and supervision

My research began in pure mathematics, especially hyperbolic geometry and the
topology of locally symmetric spaces. My current interests connect geometry,
computation, optimization, quantum error correction, and mathematics education.
They include hyperbolic surface codes, QUBO formulations, Rauzy fractals,
quantum simulators, and automated university timetabling.

An important part of my work is finding research questions that young
researchers can genuinely enter. I have supervised project groups through
DGIST's Undergraduate Group Research Program and worked with gifted
high-school students in R&amp;E and Pre-URP programs. Several of these projects
developed into publications, including work on efficient QUBO formulations for
graph colouring and on the boundary of the Rauzy fractal.
